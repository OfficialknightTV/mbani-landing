01 / THE STORY

One request is easy to forget. Three are worth a closer look.

Imagine you run a small consultancy. In one week, three clients ask if you can handle their monthly reporting. One asks on a call, one sends a message, and one drops it into a meeting. By Friday, someone needs to decide whether this is a service you should offer.

The monthly reporting pilot Illustrative scenario
  1. 01 / THE SIGNAL

    Three requests. Three different needs.

    One client wants a board summary. Another wants a sales dashboard. A third is interested but hasn’t confirmed a budget. There’s a pattern, and there’s plenty you still don’t know.

    What you heard

    “Could you pull these numbers together for our board every month?”

  2. 02 / THE QUESTION

    Decide what you need to learn.

    Before building a new offering, pick the smallest question you can answer: would two clients actually try the same report? Give it an owner, a deadline, and a clear way to judge the result.

    The brief

    Can we offer one useful report to two clients using the data they already have?

  3. 03 / THE WORK

    Get a proposal the team can review.

    Start a mission in MBANI Agent. Use the three requests to compare needs, sketch the smallest useful report, and draft a pilot. The team can see what the draft assumes and where it needs a human answer.

    Draft for review
    1. One report format
    2. Two clients to invite
    3. One question still open: who can share the data?
  4. 04 / THE DECISION

    Try the pilot. Keep the reasons.

    The team approves a small test and puts the supporting requests, the budget gap, and the follow-up date in MBANI Nexus. When the replies come in, everyone can see what changed and decide what to do next.

    Decision recorded

    Invite two clients. Revisit after the first report. Hold the wider launch until there’s stronger evidence.

The work moves from a conversation to a proposal to a decision someone else can pick up.

05 / GOOD QUESTIONS

Before you try it.

What’s the difference between Agent and Nexus?

Agent is the workspace for doing a piece of work: briefing it, following the mission, and reviewing the output. Nexus is the workspace for tracking signals, evidence, experiments, and decisions over time.

Where should I start?

Start with Agent when you have a task to move forward. Start with Nexus when you’re trying to understand what the available evidence supports. The demo follows one example across both workspaces.

Does it decide what to do for me?

In the example, Agent drafts a proposal and surfaces open questions. The team reviews the draft, makes the decision, and records its reasoning in Nexus.

Which features can I try here?

You can step through the demo and browse both interactive sample workspaces. The previews use illustrative data and do not run real missions. Features in the roadmap are marked as concepts.
